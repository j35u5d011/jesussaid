JESUSSAID

Mark 14:13-25

The Last Supper

Go into the city, and a man carrying a jar of water will meet you. Follow him. Say to the owner of the house he enters, ‘The Teacher asks: Where is my guest room, where I may eat the Passover with my disciples? He will show you a large room upstairs, furnished, and ready. Make preparations for us there. Truly I tell you, one of you will betray me – one who is eating with me. It is one of the Twelve, one who dips bread into the bowl with me. The Son of Man will go just as it is written about him. But woe to that man who betrays the Son of Man! It would be better for him if he had not been born. Take it; this is my body. This is my blood of the covenant, which is poured out for many. Truly I tell you, I will not drink again from the fruit of the vine until that day when I drink it new in the kingdom of God.”

Jesus reframes the Passover meal around his own coming death, giving the disciples a shared table as the ongoing sign of the covenant.

Context

This is a Passover. Jesus has arranged it carefully — the room, the water-carrier, the timing. The disciples sit down for a meal steeped in the memory of liberation from Egypt. He takes the bread, breaks it, and says take it, this is my body. He takes the cup and says this is my blood of the covenant, poured out for many. Then he pledges to drink it new with them in the kingdom of God. The evening carries grief — one of you will betray me — and hope in the same breath. What he institutes is not a new sacrifice. It is a shared meal. Communal. Repeatable. Simple. Bread and wine at a table, the way his community had already been eating together.
