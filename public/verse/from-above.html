From Above

You are of this world; I am not of this world. I told you that you would die in your sins; if you do not believe that I am he, you will indeed die in your sins. Just what I have been telling you from the beginning, I have much to say in judgment of you. But he who sent me is trustworthy, and what I have heard from him I tell the world.” The one who sent me is with me; he has not left me alone, for I always do what pleases him.”

Jesus tells his opponents they are operating from a different order of consciousness — one from below, one from above.

Context

The dispute in the temple courts sharpens. Jesus tells the leaders he is going away and they will look for him and not find him and will die in their sins. They wonder aloud if he plans to kill himself. Then he draws the line that names the whole problem: 'You are from below; I am from above. You are of this world; I am not of this world.' This is not a metaphysical claim about pre-existent souls. It is a description of two orders of consciousness. The 'from below' consciousness operates from ego, fear, self-protection, and the assumption that this material world is all there is. The 'from above' consciousness operates from the Father — from love, trust, alignment, and the awareness that the kingdom is present. Two people can be standing in the same courtyard and living in different worlds.
