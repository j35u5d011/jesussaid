JESUSSAID

Matthew 11:21-24

Woe on the Towns

Woe to you, Chorazin! Woe to you, Bethsaida! For if the miracles that were performed in you had been performed in Tyre and Sidon, they would have repented long ago in sackcloth and ashes. But I tell you, it will be more bearable for Tyre and Sidon on the day of judgment than for you. And you, Capernaum, will you be lifted in the heavens? No, you will go down to Hades. For if the miracles that were performed in you had been performed in Sodom, it would have remained to this day. But I tell you that it will be more bearable for Sodom on the day of judgment that for you.”

Towns that witnessed Jesus's works up close and did not respond will face a harder reckoning than pagan cities that never had the chance.

Context

This is one of the sharpest laments in the gospels, and it lands on the three towns that had seen the most. Chorazin, Bethsaida, and Capernaum sat around the north end of the Sea of Galilee — Jesus's home base. He healed there. He taught there. He walked the streets. And nothing changed. In the Jesus Way reading, the woe is not a threat of divine punishment. It is a statement about reciprocity — the same law that runs through all his teaching. What you do with what you have been given is what you get back. Tyre and Sidon were the ancient pagan trading cities that the Hebrew prophets thundered against. Sodom is the archetype of moral collapse. Jesus is saying: those cities, given what you were given, would have turned. You did not. That is a harder position to be in than never having heard.
