Deny Yourself and Follow

Whoever wants to be my disciple must deny themselves and take up their cross and follow me. For whoever wants to save their life will lose it, but whoever loses their life for me will find it. Context

Peter has just been called the rock. A few verses later he is called Satan. That is not an accident. Peter tries to talk Jesus out of the suffering road, and Jesus hears in it the exact voice he heard in the wilderness — the accuser, the ego, the human concern that wants to protect what should be spent. HaSatan is not a horned figure. It is the voice inside that says save yourself, avoid the cost, take the easier road. When it comes through Peter, Jesus names it and turns his back on it. Then the teaching. Deny yourself. Take up your cross. Follow. Aparneomai — utterly disown, break every link that ties you to yourself. The cross is not a decorative burden. In first-century Palestine it was Rome's public terror machine. Take up your cross meant walk toward death without pretending you were not walking toward it.
