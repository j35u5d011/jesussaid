The Donkey and the Colt

Jesus enters Jerusalem staging a deliberate anti-imperial parody of a conquering king, then weeps because the city cannot see what would bring it peace.

Context

The entry into Jerusalem is street theater with a very specific script. Zechariah 9:9 said the king would come to Zion humble and riding on a donkey. Roman generals entered cities on warhorses at the head of legions. Jesus enters on a borrowed colt at the head of pilgrims waving branches. Every element is chosen. This is not a spontaneous parade — it is a prophetic sign-act, in the tradition of Jeremiah smashing pots and Isaiah walking naked. The kingdom Jesus proclaims runs by the opposite logic of the empire he is walking into. If they keep quiet, he tells the Pharisees, the stones themselves will cry out. What is happening cannot be silenced because the moment is already in motion.
