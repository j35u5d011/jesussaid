Put Your Sword Back

Do what you came for, friend. Put your sword back in its place, for all who draw the sword will die by the sword. Do you think I cannot call on my Father, and he will at once put at my disposal more than twelve legions of angels? But how then would the Scriptures be fulfilled that say it must happen in this way? Am I leading a rebellion, that you have come out with swords and clubs to capture me? Every day I sat in the temple courts teaching, and you did not arrest me. But this has all taken place that the writings of the prophets might be fulfilled.”

Faced with arrest and violence, Jesus refuses armed resistance and heals the man his disciple wounded.

Context

This is the moment the whole non-violence teaching of the Sermon on the Mount is put to the test in real time. Love your enemies. Do not resist an evil person. If someone strikes you on the cheek, offer the other. It is one thing to teach it in the abstract, on a hillside, to people not currently in danger. It is another when the swords are drawn and the mob is at the door and one of your own disciples has just drawn blood. Jesus stops him. Puts the sword back. All who draw the sword will die by the sword. In Luke, he touches the wounded man and heals him — the very last healing he performs before dying.
