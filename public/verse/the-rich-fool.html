JESUSSAID

Luke 12:14-21

The Rich Fool

Man, who appointed me a judge or an arbiter between you? Watch out! Be on your guard against all kinds of greed; life does not consist in an abundance of possessions. The ground of a certain rich man yielded an abundant harvest. He thought to himself, ‘What shall I do? I have no place to store my crops.’ Then he said, ‘This is what I’ll do. I will tear down my barns and build bigger ones, and there I will store my surplus grain.’ And I’ll say to myself, ‘You have plenty of grain laid up for many years. Take life easy; eat, drink and be merry.’ But God said to him, ‘You fool! This very night your life will be demanded from you. Then who will get what you have prepared for yourself?’ This is how it will be with whoever stores up things for themselves but is not rich toward God.”

A life is not the size of its storehouse; hoarding for yourself while ignoring God and neighbor is the definition of foolishness.

Context

A man in the crowd tries to enlist Jesus as an inheritance arbitrator. Jesus refuses the role and turns the moment into a diagnosis of the underlying disease — greed, and the illusion that a life is measured by what it accumulates. The parable is almost cinematic. A good harvest, a private monologue, a plan to demolish and rebuild bigger, a self-congratulatory retirement speech to his own soul. Take life easy. Eat, drink, be merry. Then one line from God undoes it all: You fool. Tonight your life will be demanded. Who gets the surplus now? The rich man is not condemned for being productive. He is condemned for the pronouns. My crops, my barns, my grain, my soul. Not a single mention of God, and not a single mention of a neighbor.
