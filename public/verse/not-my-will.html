Luke 22:40-46

Not My Will

Pray that you will not fall into temptation. Father, if you are willing, take this cup from me; yet not my will, but yours be done. Why are you sleeping? Get up and pray so that you will not fall into temptation.”

The prayer Jesus modelled in his hardest hour is the same one he taught the disciples — align your will with the Father's, and stay awake.

Context

The hour has arrived and Jesus knows it. He goes to the Mount of Olives, tells the disciples to pray so they will not fall into temptation, and walks a stone's throw away to pray alone. The prayer itself is stripped bare of theology. Father, if you are willing, take this cup from me — the honest human ask. Yet not my will, but yours be done — the surrender. This is exactly what he taught them to pray in the Lord's Prayer: thy will be done, on earth as it is in heaven. Here he prays it in the flesh, in a garden, with sweat like blood, before anyone has laid a hand on him. He doesn't ask to be exempt from what is coming. He asks that God's will be what happens through him.
