JESUSSAID

Luke 10:26-37

The Good Samaritan

What is written in the Law? How do you read it? You have answered correctly, do this and you will live. A man was going down from Jerusalem to Jericho, when he was attacked by robbers. They stripped him of his clothes, beat him and went away, leaving him half dead. A priest happened to be going down the same road, and when he saw the man, he passed by on the other side. So too, a Levite, when he came to the place and saw him, passed by on the other side. But a Samaritan, as he traveled, came where the man was; and when he saw him, he took pity on him. He went to him and bandaged his wounds, pouring on oil and wine. Then he put the man on his own donkey, brought him to an inn and took care of him. The next day he took out two denarii and gave them to the innkeeper. ‘Look after him, and when I return, I will reimburse you for any extra expense you may have.’ Which of these three do you think was a neighbor to the man who fell into the hands of robbers? Go and do likewise.”

Being a neighbor is defined by acts of mercy across every line that would otherwise divide you from the person in front of you.

Context

A lawyer wants a clean definition — who is my neighbor? Jesus refuses to draw the line the lawyer is asking for. Instead he tells a story. A man is beaten and left half-dead. A priest and a Levite — the religious professionals, the ones who know the law — walk past on the other side of the road. A Samaritan, one of the group Jews of the day held in contempt, stops. He binds the wounds, uses his own oil and wine, puts the man on his own animal, pays for his care, and promises to come back. Then Jesus flips the question. He does not answer who is my neighbor. He asks: which of these three was a neighbor? The lawyer cannot even bring himself to say the word Samaritan — he says the one who showed mercy. And Jesus says: go and do likewise.
