JESUSSAID

Matthew 23:2-12

One Teacher

’The teachers of the law and the Pharisees sit in Moses’ seat. So you must be careful to do everything they tell you. But do not do what they do, for they do not practice what they preach. They tie up heavy, cumbersome loads and put them on other people’s shoulders, but they themselves are not willing to lift a finger to move them. Everything they do is done for people to see: They make their phylacteries wide and the tassels on their garments long; they love the place of honor at banquets and the mist important seats in the synagogues; they love to be greeted with respect in the marketplaces and to be called ‘Rabbi’ by others. But you are not to be called ‘Rabbi,’ for you have one Teacher, and you are all brothers. And do not call anyone on earth ‘father,’ for you have one Father, and he is in heaven. Nor are you to be called instructors, for you have one Instructor, the Messiah. The greatest among you will be your servant. For those who exalt themselves will be humbled, and those who humble themselves will be exalted.”

Reject religious titles and hierarchy — you have one Teacher and one Father, and you are all brothers.

Context

Jesus is not attacking Moses's seat or the substance of the Torah. He tells the crowds to do what the scribes and Pharisees say, because they occupy the teaching office. But do not do what they do. They pile heavy loads on other people's shoulders and lift not a finger to help. They dress for status — wider phylacteries, longer tassels. They love the head table, the front seat in the synagogue, the deferential greeting in the marketplace, the title Rabbi. Jesus's diagnosis is precise: everything they do is done to be seen. The religious system has become a stage for performance, and the performers demand honor from an audience that is being crushed under the weight of the show.
