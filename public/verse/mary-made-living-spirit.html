JESUSSAID

Gospel of Thomas 114

Mary Made Living Spirit

Simon Peter said to him, "Let Mary leave us, for women are not worthy of life." Jesus said, "I myself shall lead her in order to make her male, so that she too may become a living spirit resembling you males. For every woman who will make herself male will enter the kingdom of heaven." The Gospel According to Thomas

Jesus defends Mary against Peter's exclusion and declares that every woman who awakens the living spirit within enters the kingdom.

Context

This is the most controversial saying in Thomas, and the most misread. Peter says let Mary leave us, for women are not worthy of life — a straight statement of the patriarchal assumption of the era. Jesus refuses. He says he himself will lead her, so that she too becomes a living spirit like the male disciples. In first-century language, male was code for the fully-realized human — active, awake, participating. Female, in this same rhetorical frame, meant the un-awakened, the receptive-only, the not-yet. Jesus is not erasing Mary. He is dignifying her. He is telling Peter that she has the same access to becoming a living spirit that any of them do. The saying is Jesus's refusal to let his community exclude women — a refusal consistent with everything the gospels report about him.
