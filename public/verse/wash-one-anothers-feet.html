JESUSSAID

John 13:7-17

Wash One Another's Feet

You do not realize now what I am doing, but later you will understand. Unless I wash you, you have no part with me. Those who have had a bath need only to wash their feet; their whole body is clean. And you are clean, though not every one of you. Do you understand what I have done for you? You call me ‘Teacher” Now that I, your Lord and Teacher, have washed your feet, you also should wash one another’s feet. I have set you an example that you should do as I have done for you. Very truly I tell you, no servant is greater than his master, nor is a messenger greater than the one who sent him. Now that you know these things, you will be blessed if you do them.”

Greatness in Jesus's community means taking the lowest servant's role and doing it for each other.

Context

In first-century Palestine, foot-washing was slave work. Guests arrived with the road on their feet; a household slave rinsed them clean at the door. If no slave was available, the lowest-status person present did it. In this scene the Lord and Teacher of the community picks up the basin and does it himself. Then he says the words that reorganize everything: I have set you an example that you should do as I have done for you. This is the ritual that never took hold. Christianity kept the meal and largely dropped the basin. Yet here Jesus makes it explicit — this is what discipleship looks like.
