JESUSSAID

John 16:16-33

Your Grief Will Turn to Joy

In a little while you will see me no more, and then after a little while you will see me. In a little while you will see me no more, and then after a little while you will see me. Because I am going to the Father. Are you asking one another what I meant when I said, ‘In a little while you will see me not more, and then after a little while you will see me’? Very truly I tell you, you will weep and mourn while the world rejoices. You will grieve, but your grief will turn to joy. A woman giving birth to a child has pain because her time has come; but when her baby is born she forgets the anguish because of her joy that a child is born into the world. So with you: Now is your time of grief, but I will see you again and you will rejoice, and no one will take away your joy. In that day you will no longer ask me anything. Very truly I tell you, my Father will give you whatever you ask in my name. Until now you have not asked for anything in my name. Ask and you will receive, and your joy will be complete. Though I have been speaking figuratively, a time is coming when I will no longer use this kind of language but will tell you plainly about my Father. In that day you will ask in my name. I am not saying that I will ask the Father on your behalf. No, the Father himself loves you because you have loved me and have believed that I came from God. I came from the Father and entered the world; now I am leaving the world and going back to the Father. Do you now believe? A time is coming and in fact has come when you will be scattered, each to your own home. You will leave me all alone. Yet I am not alone, for my Father is with me. I have told you these things, so that in me you may have peace. In this world you will have trouble. But take heart! I have overcome the world.”

The grief the disciples are about to feel is labor pain — real, brief, and followed by a joy no one can take.

Context

The image is not accidental. Jesus reaches for a woman in labor to describe what his community is about to walk through. The pain is not denied; the woman genuinely suffers. But the suffering has a direction and an end — and once the child is born, the anguish is swallowed by joy. Applied to what is coming in the next twenty-four hours, this is astonishing pastoral care. He is telling them that the crucifixion is not a random catastrophe. Something is being born through it. Their grief will not be the last word.
