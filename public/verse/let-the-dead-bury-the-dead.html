How They All Heard It

Luke

Luke 9:58-62

Foxes have dens and birds have nests, but the Son of Man has no place to lay his head. Follow Me. Let the dead bury their own dead, but you go and proclaim the kingdom of God. No one who puts a hand to the plow and looks back is fit for service in the kingdom of God.”

Context

Two would-be disciples come forward, and Jesus answers both with the same uncomfortable clarity. To the first: the Son of Man has no place to lay his head. The itinerant teacher owns nothing and offers nothing to those who join him. If you need a nest, this is not the road. To the second, who asks to first bury his father — one of the most sacred obligations in Jewish life, ranking above nearly every other duty in the Torah — Jesus says let the dead bury their own dead. The phrase is deliberately shocking. The spiritually dead can attend to the physically dead. If you have heard the call, that call is more urgent than the culture's holiest custom. Luke's version adds the third: no one who puts a hand to the plow and looks back is fit for service in the kingdom. Ploughing is present-tense work. The furrow only stays straight if your eyes stay forward.
