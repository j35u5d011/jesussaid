JESUSSAID

Luke 11:2-13

Ask, Seek, Knock

When you pray, say: Father, hallowed be your name, your kingdom come. Give us each day our daily bread. Forgive us our sins, for we also forgive everyone who sins against us. And lead us not into temptation. Suppose you have a friend, and you go to him at midnight and say, ‘Friend, lend me three loaves of bread; a friend of mine on a journey has come to me, and I have no food to offer him.’ And suppose the one inside answers, ‘Don’t bother me. The door is already locked, and my children and I hare in bed. I can’t get up and give you anything.’ I tell you, even though he will not get up and give you the bread because of friendship, yet because of your shameless audacity he will surely get up and give you as much as you need. So I say to you: Ask and it will be given to you; seek and you will find; knock and the door will be opened to you. For everyone who asks receives; the one who seeks finds; and to the one who knocks, the door will be opened. Which of you fathers, if your son asks for a fish, will give him a snake instead? Or if he asks for an egg, will give him a scorpion? If you then, though you are evil, know how to give good gifts to your children, how much more will your Father in heaven give the Holy Spirit to those who ask him!”

Prayer is persistent trust that the Father gives good gifts to those who keep asking with an open heart.

Context

Luke's version of the Lord's Prayer is shorter and rougher than Matthew's, closer to the original. Father, hallowed be your name. Kingdom come. Daily bread. Forgive as we forgive. Do not lead us into temptation. That is it. Then Jesus tells a story about a friend at midnight who bangs on the door until he gets bread. Not because the sleeping neighbor loves him but because he is shameless — the Greek is anaideia, unflinching persistence. And then the promise: ask and you will receive; seek and you will find; knock and it will be opened. The Greek verbs are continuous. Keep asking. Keep seeking. Keep knocking. Prayer is a posture, not a transaction.
